### Planning for evaluations
Whilst we still had the website underdevelopment, we started planning usability testing for the features that were becoming functional. Whilst we had events still under the wraps, we had create events, forum and map interactions, we wanted to observe if these features worked as intended. We planned goals and success metrics as a group for individual testing, whilst we know we don’t have a lot of time left to do thorough testing, it's important that the prototype should be evaluated with users. We planned to do this with at least 1-2 users before submitting our prototype to ensure we could make any iterations needed to improve the interaction flow, and ensure that their experience during the website is seamless between features. 


Whilst it was a consideration to ‘wizard of oz’ wireframes, we were happy with our progress in our prototype where we found it fitting to use the ‘think aloud’ method as our form of usability testing. This would be conducted informally and we would input our pain-points/ areas of iteration to the figjam board and create any last minute tickets needed for additional fixes.

Specifically for the map feature I built, I wanted to test if the interactive map was working well and if users could still identify the information they needed without it.  This aligned with the fallback of displaying the court information on the right if the map ever went into issues if it didn’t load properly, for accessibility considerations. The next part of testing would also involve testing against WCAG, ensuring colour choices have a good enough contrast against the 2.1 AA standards. 


The progress the team and I have made within the previous few weeks feels fast but rewarding, pushing past the brief to create something I am personally passionate about. I find myself resonating and including features that I would appreciate as a user. Building these features to not only help people connect in the community, but also help others feel more interested in the sport was something we stuck to as a goal during the project. The decisions to drop reviews and add additional security measurements to profiles, may not be something our users would’ve considered but feels rewarding as a designer to create trade-offs to improve the core purpose of the project. Whilst there is a time crunch to iterate further, we hope to add additional ‘good-to-have’ features afterwards in the upcoming weeks to complete our product.
